The High-Dimensional Challenge

  • Neuro-Fuzzy Systems suffer from the Curse of Dimensionality as rules grow exponentially with 27+ features.
  • During our early analysis, we observed that isolated pre-processing techniques (like PCA) destroy fuzzy interpretability.
  • Our Focus: We reproduced the ADAR dual-weighting mechanism to prove that models can actively build and prune their own architectures during gradient descent.

Dynamic Rule Growth Flow

  1. Monitor Validation Loss for stagnation (patience = 20 epochs).
  2. If plateaued, identify the data coordinate with the Maximum Residual Error.
  3. Spawn a new Fuzzy Rule exactly at that coordinate.
  4. Re-initialize Optimizer to adapt to the new structural parameters.

Key Insight: Why ADAR?

Why is ADAR a better engineering choice than a static ANFIS model for high-dimensional IoT data?

  • Interpretability Intact: Static ANFIS requires pre-processing feature drops, whereas ADAR preserves the semantic meaning of inputs.
  • Automated Scaling: ADAR autonomously calibrates rule quantity to data complexity, eliminating manual grid-searches for optimal structure size.
  • Hardware Efficiency: By structurally pruning ~40% of inputs mid-training, ADAR trims computational overhead dynamically.

Reflections & Limitations

  • Growth Patience: We initially struggled with the growth threshold, but found that a patience parameter of 20-50 yielded the most stable pruning results.
  • Thresholds: We opted for an attribute pruning threshold of $\theta_{attr} = 0.25$ because it securely isolated dead features while preserving variance.
  • Limitations: Hard-pruning is irreversible. A soft-pruning decay might be a safer extension for extreme non-linear datasets.
